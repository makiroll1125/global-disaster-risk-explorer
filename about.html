GLOBAL DISASTER RISK EXPLORER / METHODS

Keep the context.

A data visualization project by Tobias Garcia and Ha Nguyen, comparing reported human and economic disaster impacts across two periods.

The dataset

The supplied EM-DAT export from CRED / UCLouvain is dated 15 September 2026. It contains 10,896 country-disaster records, representing 9,001 event identifiers and 220 countries and territories, with start years 2000-2026. One record describes a disaster's impact in one country. Cross-border events appear more than once.

The original workbook, cleaned CSV, dashboard JSON and quality audit are included. Cleaning validates identifiers, geography, dates and numerical fields. Damage is converted from thousands of dollars to dollars; adjusted amounts retain the 2025 price basis. Missing impacts remain unknown rather than becoming zero.

How to read the comparison

The opening view compares 2000-2005 with 2020-2025. Annual averages divide each period's reported totals by six calendar years. Percentage changes use unrounded values. The default comparison excludes partial 2026.

Reported annual deaths fall 28.8%, affected totals fall 46.8% and adjusted damage rises 54.4%. These differences describe the selected records and periods. They do not establish a continuous trend or explain why the differences occurred. Individual extreme events and reporting practices influence totals.

Disaster-type selection applies across charts. Year ranges and country selections are local to their views. The opening cards remain worldwide under the selected disaster-type filters; country selection updates the comparison detail tables. The archive summary and downloadable CSV cover all years, including partial 2026.

Important limitations

  • Only 17.1% of records have source coordinates. Earthquakes have far higher coordinate coverage than storms; droughts, epidemics and extreme-temperature records have none. Point density cannot represent all disasters. Hollow references identify countries, not event locations.
  • Country and regional totals include records without coordinates. The 279 records without matching boundary polygons remain in tables and other summaries.
  • Deaths are reported in 71.8% of archive records, affected totals in 80.9% and adjusted damage in 30.3%. Coverage varies between periods. Unknown values do not mean no impact.
  • Affected totals can count the same people across events. Counts are not adjusted for population or exposure, and the data do not estimate future risk.
  • Replay includes only complete valid start dates through the export cutoff. 2026 remains partial and has no adjusted damage values.
